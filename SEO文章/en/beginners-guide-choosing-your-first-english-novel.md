# Reading Original English Books: How to Choose Your First Novel

You bought *Harry Potter*, gave up after three pages. You bought *The Great Gatsby*, and page two was nothing but unknown words. This happens to nearly every English learner. **When your first original English book doesn't work out, it's usually not that you're unsuited to original reading — it's that the difficulty didn't match.** This guide gives you a repeatable method for choosing a book, plus books that genuinely work for beginners.

## First, the 5 Things That Decide a Choice

### 1. Lexile Level

Lexile is the standard measure of text difficulty, from 0L up to 2000L+. Roughly:

- **Children's / young-adult fiction: 400L–800L** — suitable for a solid college-bound reader.
- **Contemporary popular fiction: 800L–1000L** — suitable for upper-intermediate readers.
- **Classic literature: 1000L–1300L+** — dense with unknown words and long sentences.

### 2. Vocabulary Difficulty (the most overlooked)

Lexile measures sentence structure; what actually trips you up is **density of unknown words**. Two books at the same Lexile can differ enormously in vocabulary. The reliable way to check is a **word-frequency analysis**: look at the CET4 / CET6 coverage of the book. Below ~95%, reading will be a slog.

### 3. Length

Don't start with a doorstopper. A book under 300 pages is finishable within two to three weeks, which keeps you going.

### 4. Interest (what decides whether you finish)

No matter how well the difficulty matches, you won't finish a book you don't enjoy. Pick a genre you already like — mystery, romance, sci-fi, or fantasy.

### 5. Tense and Style

Books with conversational, modern prose are far easier than archaic or literary language. At the beginner stage, avoid works from the 19th century and earlier.

## "Skim It First, Analyze It Second"

Don't order on cover art and recommendations alone. Confirm in two steps:

1. **The skim test**: open the first two pages and count unknown words. More than 5–7 per page is a strong sign the level is too high.
2. **The frequency test (more accurate)**: upload the ebook as `.txt` to **WordLoom** and check two numbers:
   - **CET4 / CET6 coverage**: below 95% means too many unknown words — shelve it for now;
   - **High-frequency share**: if the most frequent 2,000 words account for a large share, the book's vocabulary is basic and good for stepping up.

## A Suggested Progression

- **Book 1**: choose a children's or YA title (*Charlotte's Web*, *Wonder*), with the goal of *finishing*, not understanding every word.
- **Books 2–3**: move to popular fiction (*The Hunger Games*, *Harry Potter*), chasing fluent reading.
- **After that**: pick harder books by interest, and keep tracking your coverage with frequency analysis to watch it climb.

## A Closing Word

Choosing a book isn't about picking "the best book" — it's about picking **a book you can actually finish**. When the difficulty matches and the interest is there, original reading sticks. Use a frequency analyzer to quantify each book's true difficulty, and you never have to guess again.
