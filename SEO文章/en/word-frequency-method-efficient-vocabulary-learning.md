# The Word-Frequency Method: Learn Vocabulary the Efficient Way

How many times have you started at "abandon"? Open almost any vocabulary book and it's the very first word — and it's where most people give up. **Inefficient vocabulary learning is usually not a matter of effort but of method.** The most common mistake is "equal effort": spending your energy on both the highest-frequency words and rare words you'll never use. Here's a frequency-based method that puts your effort where it counts.

## Why Frequency Matters

English has hundreds of thousands of words, but they're used wildly unevenly — a classic long tail:

- **The 1,000 most frequent words** cover about 75–80% of ordinary text;
- **The top 2,000** cover about 90%;
- **The top 3,000** cover about 93–95%;
- Beyond that, each word adds less than 0.1% of coverage.

What this means: **master the top 2,000–3,000 words and you can understand the vast majority of everyday English.** More often, what you need is to fill in the "situation-specific" words while reading. The core of the word-frequency method, then, is to **order words by how often they're used and master the high-frequency ones first** — instead of going alphabetically or spending equal effort across a whole book.

## The 4 Steps of the Frequency Method

### Step 1: Attack the High-Frequency Words First

Whether you're preparing for CET, IELTS, or just improving your reading, **start with the most frequent words.** High-frequency words are your foundation. Generate a list sorted by frequency, work from top to bottom, and mark the ones you don't know.

### Step 2: Reinforce in Real Context

Learning words in isolation comes with a shocking forget rate. The right way is to **learn a word inside a real example sentence** — its collocations, its usage, its multiple senses. This beats rote-memorizing dictionary definitions, because your brain remembers *how the word is used*, not just a symbol.

### Step 3: Filter Precisely by Exam Level

If your goal is an exam, this is where quantification pays off. Run a **frequency analysis** on a novel or real test corpus, then filter by **CET4 / CET6 / postgraduate / IELTS** tags. You'll discover that:

- many words you thought were hard never appear in the high-frequency zone of your target exam;
- some "easy" words from middle school are, in real text, the most frequent core words of all.

Filtering by tags gives you a list that matches your goal precisely — no blind studying of a whole word book.

### Step 4: Spaced Repetition

Use the forgetting curve: review on day 1, day 3, day 7… and tie each review back to the original example sentence. Review what you know, and learn something new.

## Common Mistakes

- **Mistake 1: studying A→Z.** Rare words eat your energy; high-frequency words never get cemented.
- **Mistake 2: words without example sentences.** A word with no context hangs alone in memory and is soon forgotten.
- **Mistake 3: chasing a "vocabulary number."** What decides real reading is coverage — what percentage of a text you recognize — not a raw total.
- **Mistake 4: no review.** Without spaced repetition, even the best list goes to waste.

## Summary

The essence of the word-frequency method is to **spend your limited energy on the words used most often, and reinforce them in real context.** It's not about memorizing a hundred thousand words; it's about remembering the words you'll actually use.

**Next time you open a vocabulary book, ask yourself first: which words do I actually need? Use a frequency analyzer and let the data answer.**
